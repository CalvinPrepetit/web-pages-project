Who I Am

A person shaped more by experience than theory alone.

I have never been drawn to learning just for the sake of collecting information. I value knowledge that can be applied, tested under pressure, and turned into something useful. That mindset shaped the way I approached sports, military service, telecom work, construction, business, and now enterprise technology.

Over time, my definition of success changed. I still care deeply about growth and high standards, but I no longer measure success only by output or income. I care just as much about whether the life I am building leaves room to coach my kids, support my wife, and actually be present where it counts.

Music is another major part of my life. I play piano, guitar, violin, bajo quinto, accordion, trumpet, and harmonica, and I have always appreciated the discipline, creativity, and feel for timing that music develops.

What Drives Me

Useful skill, dependable work, steady growth, and meaningful time with family.

What I respect

Trades, technical depth, leadership under pressure, and the kind of work that leaves something solid behind.

The Road So Far

A career built in layers

01

Sports and early leadership

I played baseball, hockey, golf, chess, and wrestled growing up, and I had the opportunity to captain both the wrestling and hockey teams, along with serving as captain of the chess team in middle school. Those experiences helped shape my leadership, discipline, and ability to stay steady when expectations are high.

02

Marine Corps communications

After graduating from Philip O'Berry Academy of Technology, I joined the United States Marine Corps and served in communications as an 0612 and 0613 wireman. That work gave me hands-on experience with Cisco equipment, Redcom systems, switching, fiber optic splicing, aerial and underground line, and the kind of mission-ready thinking that stays with you. I also graduated at the top of my class in physical fitness and academics, earning multiple recognitions that reflected both discipline and performance.

03

Telecom and tower work

After the military, I moved into contract work through Dish Network and Spectrum, managing my own inventory van, handling a demanding daily workload, and working directly with the systems people rely on in their homes. I also climbed cell towers, which expanded my understanding of the network from the customer side all the way to the infrastructure that keeps everything connected.

04

Construction and ownership

I later moved into construction, working in paint, steel erection, upfits, staircases, and railings. I eventually ran my own steel erection business and managed the warehouse of a separate paint business, which gave me a deeper understanding of operations, logistics, leadership, and the daily weight of carrying real responsibility.

05

Changing direction for the right reasons

After my second child, I realized I did not want success to mean working seven days a week and missing the life I was trying to provide for. I wanted a path that still rewarded skill and hard work, but gave me more of my time back in the moments that mattered most.

06

Cisco, specialization, and growth

I entered Cisco through the Veteran Talent Incubation Program, earned both my CCNA and AITECH certification, and stepped into the corporate technology world with a strong foundation and a willingness to learn fast. I grew quickly on the telepresence team working with CUCM, Expressways, endpoints, and collaboration systems, then moved into security and specialized in firewalls. Today I work as a High Touch Technical Support engineer for the Amazon account, reviewing scripts, diagnosing network issues, and helping keep critical operations stable and dependable.

Work Now

Enterprise-scale engineering with practical impact

My work today sits at the intersection of networking, security, troubleshooting, review, and operational resilience. What I enjoy most is that the work demands clarity: understand the system, identify the risk, find the real failure point, and help move toward the right solution.

I have also helped create artificial intelligence efforts within my team, which fits naturally with the way I think about process improvement and smarter problem-solving.

Education Now

Formal learning has become part of the plan

I am currently studying Artificial Intelligence at Central Piedmont Community College. Earlier in life, I did not have much room to prioritize traditional education because responsibility required me to focus on knowledge that could improve the next day, the next year, or the next decade in immediate ways.

Now I see formal education differently. I still value practical skill first, but I also appreciate the structure, discipline, and perspective that school can add. My goal is to remain a lifelong learner and model that for my children too.

The Life Behind the Resume

What all of this work supports

My wife and I have been together since high school, and my family is the center of how I think about time, work, and long-term goals. A big part of why I changed direction professionally was so I could be present for the life I was building with them.

My role now

Outside of work, I coach my children, support their events, and serve as a scout leader with my son. Those parts of my life are not separate from success to me. They are the reason success matters.

Where I'm Going

Keep building a life where technical depth, useful work, and family presence can all coexist.

I want to keep growing in networking, security, automation, and AI. I want to keep developing as a reviewer, engineer, mentor, and lifelong learner. Most of all, I want the path ahead to keep reflecting the same values that shaped the path behind me: practicality, discipline, growth, and showing up for the people who matter most.
