What Shapes My Story

I have never been someone who learns just for the sake of collecting information. I value knowledge that can be applied, tested, and put to work. That mindset shaped the way I approached the military, telecom, construction, business, and now technology at a much deeper level.

Over time, my definition of success changed too. I moved away from chasing endless work and toward building a life where I could still provide well while being present for my family in the moments that matter most.

From High School to the Marine Corps

After graduating from Philip O'Berry Academy of Technology, I chose a path that gave me structure, challenge, and the chance to grow quickly. I joined the United States Marine Corps, where I learned the values of discipline, accountability, resilience, and teamwork.

In the Marine Corps, I served in communications as an 0612 and 0613 wireman. That work put me in environments where communications had to stay reliable, organized, and mission-ready. My job involved helping build and maintain the systems that kept people connected, often under tight timelines and demanding conditions.

Hands-On Technical Experience

My military communications work gave me direct experience with Cisco equipment, Redcom systems, switching, data infrastructure, and the real mechanics behind making communications dependable. I worked with fiber optic splicing, phone connections, aerial line, and underground line, and I spent time close to both the data and radio sides of the field.

One of the things I still appreciate most about that time is how practical it was. We could set up communications quickly, tear them down, and rebuild again as needed. It taught me how to think under pressure while staying focused on the mission.

Telecom Work After the Military

After leaving the Marine Corps, I chose work that could keep my family moving forward right away. I did contract work through Dish Network and Spectrum, managing my own inventory van, handling daily jobs, and working directly in customers' homes and service environments.

I also climbed cell towers, which added another layer to my understanding of how networks operate from end to end. That is part of why I often say I have touched just about every part of the network, from in the house to high above it.

Construction, Trade Work, and Ownership

I later moved into construction, where I worked in paint, steel erection, staircases, railings, upfits, and the demanding rhythm of full-day physical work. That season of my life strengthened my respect for skilled labor and reinforced how much I value knowing how to build with my hands.

I eventually started a steel erection business and also managed the warehouse of a separate paint business to better understand large-scale construction operations. That gave me experience not only in the field, but in responsibility, logistics, and business management.

Changing Direction on Purpose

Even though the work was successful, it demanded a level of time away from family that I no longer wanted for my life. By then, I was thinking more seriously about how I wanted to spend my time, not just how much I could work.

That shift pushed me back toward communications and technology, a field I had always enjoyed and respected. I wanted a path that still rewarded skill and effort, but also gave me more room to be present as a husband and father.

Cisco, Growth, and the Work I Do Now

Using the experience I had built over the years, along with the recognition I earned in the Marine Corps and telecom industry, I worked my way into Cisco's Veterans Talent Program. After a year of study and earning my CCNA, I was able to step into a new chapter of my career.

At Cisco, I first worked on the telepresence team and became a subject matter expert in multiple collaboration technologies. I was later asked to move into the security team, where I specialized in firewalls, continued building technical depth, and showed that I could contribute at a higher level across both operations and development-minded work.

That growth opened the door for me to become a high-touch engineer for the Amazon account. In that role, the work goes beyond routine support. It means helping keep major corporate operations and fulfillment infrastructure dependable so that the systems people rely on every day continue to perform.

Current Education

My current major at CPCC is Artificial Intelligence. College was never something that naturally pulled me in because I have always leaned toward practical learning. I like understanding things that can be built, used, fixed, improved, or turned into something real.

At this point in my life, though, I have started to see school in a more balanced way. Even when academic work feels far from the kind of projects I would normally choose on my own, it still stretches my thinking and helps me grow.

Plans for Further Education

I plan to remain a lifelong learner. For me, that means continuing formal education where it adds value, staying open to certifications, and always being willing to learn the next tool, system, or skill that can make me more effective.

I have learned that education is not limited to one setting. Some lessons come from school, some from work, some from building things, and some from the responsibility of leading a family. All of it matters if it helps shape a stronger, wiser person.

Career Plans

My career plan is to keep growing at the intersection of networking, security, automation, and practical problem-solving. I want to continue deepening my technical skill while also expanding the impact I can have through leadership, code review, systems thinking, and sound engineering judgment.

I am especially interested in work that turns knowledge into real results, whether that means designing, securing, reviewing, or improving the systems people depend on every day.

Interests and Personal Values

A lot of what I enjoy comes back to learning by doing. I respect trades, tools, systems, and skills that create something useful in the world. Whether it is wiring, fiber, construction, networking, or development work, I appreciate the satisfaction that comes from understanding how something works and then making it work well.

More than anything, I value growth with purpose. I want my work, education, and skills to support a meaningful life, strong family presence, and a future built on both capability and character.
