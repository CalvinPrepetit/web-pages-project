Where I Started

Practical from the beginning

I graduated from Philip O'Berry Academy of Technology in Charlotte, but even early on I was wired to value hands-on learning. I have always respected knowledge you can apply immediately, whether that means building, leading, fixing, securing, or improving something in front of you.

What Grounds Me

Family changed my definition of success

My wife and I have been together since high school, and my children have shaped nearly every major decision I have made as an adult. I still care deeply about growth and achievement, but not at the cost of being absent from the life I am working so hard to build.

The Road So Far

A path built in real environments

01

Sports and leadership

Growing up, I played baseball, hockey, golf, and wrestled. I later became captain of both the wrestling team and the hockey team, which taught me discipline, accountability, and how to lead under pressure.

02

Marine Corps communications

After high school, I joined the United States Marine Corps. I served in communications as an 0612 and 0613 wireman, working with systems that had to stay organized, reliable, and mission ready. That experience sharpened my work ethic and gave me direct exposure to Cisco equipment, Redcom systems, switching, fiber, and communications infrastructure.

03

Telecom in the field

After the military, I stayed close to work that could create immediate value. I handled contract work through Dish Network and Spectrum, managed my own inventory van, worked directly in customer environments, and climbed cell towers. That period gave me a broader understanding of the network from the home all the way up to the tower.

04

Construction and ownership

I later moved into construction, doing steel erection, paint work, railings, staircases, upfits, and other physically demanding work. I eventually ran my own steel erection business and also managed the warehouse of a separate paint business, which taught me as much about responsibility and operations as it did about trade work itself.

05

Returning to tech with purpose

After my second child, I made a major decision to redirect my life. Running nonstop might have made sense on paper, but it was not the life I wanted long term. I wanted to keep building a meaningful career while also being more present for my family, so I returned to the communications and technology path.

06

Cisco, specialization, and impact

I entered Cisco through the Venture Talent Incubation Program, earned my CCNA, and stepped into corporate technology. I first grew within telepresence and collaboration, working across CUCM, Expressways, endpoints, and the systems that keep people connected. I was later moved into security, where I specialized in firewalls, deepened my technical range, and built a strong reputation in script review, issue diagnosis, and large-scale support.

What I Do Now

High-touch engineering with real stakes

Today I work as a High Touch Technical Support engineer supporting the Amazon account at Cisco. That means helping protect and maintain systems tied to major corporate and fulfillment operations where stability, resilience, and speed matter. On any given day I may be reviewing scripts, digging into network behavior, tracing the source of a problem, or helping improve the quality of what customers rely on.

I have also helped build artificial intelligence efforts within my team to improve workflows and strengthen how we solve problems. That blend of networking, security, review, and practical automation fits the way I naturally think.

Why Education Matters Now

Formal learning has new value for me

I am currently studying Artificial Intelligence at Central Piedmont Community College. For most of my life, practical knowledge had to come first because time, responsibility, and family did not leave room for learning that felt too distant from real results. Now that I have more stability in the technology field, I can appreciate formal education differently.

I still believe in hands-on learning, but I also see the value in academic growth, structure, and long-term development. My goal is to remain a lifelong learner and show my children that steady learning can be just as important as practical skill.

What Matters Most

The life behind the work

Family

The biggest reward of changing career direction has been getting more meaningful time with my wife and children. I coach their teams, show up for their events, and serve as a scout leader with my son. That time matters more to me than any version of success that keeps me away from them.

Useful skill

I have always respected work that creates something tangible. Whether it is wiring, fiber, networking, code review, troubleshooting, or building with my hands, I find meaning in learning skills that can be put to work immediately.

Growth

I do not see growth as a one-time achievement. I want to keep improving in networking, security, automation, and AI while also growing as a father, husband, mentor, and teammate.

Where I'm Going

I want to keep building a life where skill, purpose, and presence all coexist.

My future plans are simple in principle, even if the work itself is demanding: keep learning, keep sharpening my technical ability, keep contributing in meaningful ways, and keep showing up for the people I love. If there is one thing my path has taught me, it is that growth means the most when it strengthens both your work and your life.
